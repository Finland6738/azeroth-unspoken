# Content waves

The plan of the content of [ADR 0024](../adr/0024-selection-index-compact-registry-and-bounded-memory.md) and the owner's goal (100,000-300,000 whole, hand-written texts per language, 400+ hours per character without an exact repetition; [ADR 0001](../adr/0001-engine-and-narrative-database-separated.md): no composition from fragments), measured by the need model of [ADR 0050](../adr/0050-need-model-near-duplicates-and-content-waves.md) ([NEED.md](NEED.md)).

A **wave** is about 2,000-3,000 texts per language: one session with parallel writer subagents, like the trial slice of PR #241. The waves fill the **soft need** of every pool first (no text twice in any 40 hours of a character's life), then the **strict need** (none in 400 hours), then the **random need**: the owner's decision of 2026-11-02 keeps today's random pick (no exhaustive memory of what was said), so a pool needs about ten times its firings for at most 5% of its interventions in 400 hours to be exact repeats, about 284,000-300,000 texts per language in all. Within a target the units go in this order: the generic lines of the most frequent events, the starting zones and the capitals, the lines of each class and each race, faction, mood, time, weather and the creature fought, the level bands, the zones by level band, the dungeons, the endgame zones, the raids and the battlegrounds; the most often met first within a kind.

The table below is generated by `python tools/archive/cli.py need --write` from what the archive holds: after a wave is merged it is regenerated, the gap shrinks and **the next wave is always the first row**. The pools of a wave (event, conditions, how many more texts) are printed by `cli need --wave W01`.

## How to run a wave

1. **Start from main.** `git fetch && git merge origin/main`, then `python tools/archive/cli.py need --write` and take the first wave of the table. `cli need --wave W01` lists its units: a place, a class, a race... with the conditions every line of the unit writes, and for each event how many more texts it needs.
2. **Authors and ids.** Every writer is an AI author of the archive named after the wave and its unit: `ai-w01-durotar`, `ai-w05-hunter` (the drafts give it as `author`; `cli drafts FILE --author ai-w05-hunter`). Ids keep the prefix of their place or layer, like the trial slice: `dur.`, `org.`, `elw.`, `sw.` for a place (a new place takes three or four letters of its name), `cls.<class>.`, `race.<race>.`, `band.<band>.`, `mood.<mood>.` for the layers, `<event>.w01.` for generic lines; then the event and a number (`cls.hunter.battle.012`).
3. **Where the lines go.** A place line goes to the regional pack of its area, one file per area in the continent folders as in PR #241 (`zone = "<zone>"` in the conditions; a capital has its own pack). A contested zone (no faction in `Data/Places.lua`) has a pool per faction: its lines also write `faction`. Class, race and the other layers go to the base packs of their family (`Data/Registry*.lua`, texts in `AzerothUnspoken_enUS`/`_itIT`). Past about 10,000 regional entries per continent and language, split the folder by area (`## X-AzerothUnspoken-Areas`, ADR 0048 and 0049).
4. **Writing.** One writer subagent per unit (or per 500 texts of a big unit), each with the brief of its unit: the event, the conditions, the number of texts, and these rules:
   - whole texts only (ADR 0001), original, in the setting, no invented proper names (CONTRIBUTING, sections 3-4); English first, then the Italian of the same lines;
   - spread the voices of the event as they are today (narrator, inner, character, npc) and the temperaments (`temperament:*` tags), registers and depths; every rule a theme and up to three motifs of the closed vocabularies;
   - no variants tagged with a depth (at the default depth only one would speak: lesson of PR #241); lines of fights, people and manual events carry `tier = 1` (a zone condition would otherwise silence the relationship and NPC lines);
   - no text `]==]`; at most one placeholder per text where the event gives it;
   - literary pieces (songs, verses, tales, formulas) only where the wave asks for them: they always go to the owner's review.
5. **Archive flow.** `cli drafts` (a refused line says why), then `cli dups --state draft`: **no exact duplicate and no near pair** that involves a draft, and no new over-used opening or closing; fix and draft again. `cli submit` the wave, `cli sample --state review --by <author> --share 0.015 --out build/review-W01.md` for each writer and hand the sheets to the owner (about 1-2% of the lines; every literary piece is on the sheet and needs the owner's steps, `cli piece`: an AI session fills the fields of a record, never a step). A "fix" or a "reject" on a sheet sends that kind of line of that writer back: redo it, then draw a new sheet. After the owner's yes: `cli approve`, `cli export`, `cli check` (round trip empty, exit 0: no exact duplicate).
6. **Checks to pass.** `python -X utf8 -I Tests/run_tests.py` until "TUTTI I CONTROLLI SUPERATI" (update the counts the tests and the README report); `cli check` and `cli dups --state approved` with no new warning; `cli report` with no intention missing a language; `python -I -X utf8 Tests/bench_trial.py --long 20` for a wave of the trial slice areas (the first repetition must not come sooner); `python -I -X utf8 tools/build_codec.py` after a big wave.
7. **Close the wave.** `cli need --write` (the table moves on), CHANGELOG (the wave, its counts, the gap left), the ROADMAP row "Content waves", a PR "Content wave W01: ..." with the folders and `authoring/` together. The parent session merges; the wiki syncs from main.
