# Phrase Packs

The lines of Azeroth Unspoken live in **phrase packs**. The addon comes with its own packs, and you can add more: a pack can bring new lines, new events, places, NPCs, dialogues, stories, or even a new language.

## What it is

A phrase pack is a small, separate addon that sits next to Azeroth Unspoken in your `AddOns` folder. It needs Azeroth Unspoken to work, and adds to it without changing it.

- **Built-in packs** come with the download: combat, exploration, life of the character, social moments, spells, classes, memory, relationships, personality, interlocutor and place, NPC dialogues, stories, frames and vocabulary. Their rules are in the `AzerothUnspoken` folder; their lines are in the language folders (`AzerothUnspoken_enUS`, `AzerothUnspoken_itIT`), and the lines tied to one continent are in the continent folders (`AzerothUnspoken_Kalimdor`, `AzerothUnspoken_EasternKingdoms` and their language folders). The addon reads only your language, and a continent only while you are there: you never pick these folders yourself. See [[Installation and Updating|Installation-and-Updating]].
- **Extra packs** are separate folders. The download includes two:
  - **`AzerothUnspoken_Example`**: a few lines for fishing and some lines tied to places;
  - **`AzerothUnspoken_Versi`**, the **verses pack** (id `versi`): a first test of literary forms, with inscriptions, remembered verses, inn rhymes, road songs, sayings, a hymn and a lament, in English and Italian. They only ever appear in your private narrator. See [[Literary Forms|Literary-Forms]].

## Places with lines of their own

Four starting areas have a large set of lines of their own, a **trial slice** to learn how much writing a place needs: **Durotar** and **Orgrimmar** for the Horde (in the Kalimdor folders), **Elwynn Forest** and **Stormwind City** for the Alliance (in the Eastern Kingdoms folders). There the narrator knows the subzones you cross (Razor Hill, Sen'jin Village, the Echo Isles; Northshire, Goldshire, the mines and the lakes; the valleys of Orgrimmar and the districts of Stormwind), the weather and the hour, the inns, the guards' directions, the creatures of the place, a death there and the way back, and who you are: an orc at home in Durotar does not hear what a human sent there hears. Races and classes change the lines; they are never separate packs.

The first content wave (W01) added the other six starting areas and capitals: **Dun Morogh** and **Ironforge**, **Teldrassil** and **Darnassus** for the Alliance, **Mulgore** and **Thunder Bluff**, **Tirisfal Glades** and **Undercity** for the Horde, with their subzones (Coldridge Valley and Kharanos, Shadowglen and Dolanaar, Camp Narache and Bloodhoof Village, Deathknell and Brill; the rises, terraces, wards and quarters of the cities), their creatures, their inns, flights and quests. It also gave every class, every race and both factions many more lines of their own (the speak key, fights, wounds, quests, heals and blessings, NPCs met), and new lines for the cities, the deserts, the jungles, the plains, the plague lands, the dark and the fire.

The second wave (W02) gave thirteen more zones lines of their own: **The Barrens**, **Darkshore**, **Ashenvale**, **Stonetalon Mountains** and **Thousand Needles** in Kalimdor; **Loch Modan**, **Westfall**, **Redridge Mountains**, **Duskwood**, **Silverpine Forest**, the **Wetlands**, the **Hillsbrad Foothills** and **Stranglethorn Vale** in the Eastern Kingdoms, with their towns (the Crossroads, Ratchet, Auberdine, Astranaar, Splintertree Post, Sun Rock Retreat, Freewind Post, Thelsamar, Sentinel Hill, Lakeshire, Darkshire, the Sepulcher, Menethil Harbor, Southshore, Tarren Mill, Grom'gol, Booty Bay...), their foes and their weather. In a zone that both sides contest (Ashenvale, Stonetalon, Thousand Needles, the Wetlands, Hillsbrad, Stranglethorn) the Alliance and the Horde have lines of their own; so far only the Alliance in the Wetlands and only the Horde in Stranglethorn. W02 also gave the character's stage of life lines of its own, from the green beginner to the veteran at the top of the levels.

The third wave (W03) reached the rest of the contested zones: **Desolace**, **Dustwallow Marsh**, **Azshara**, **Tanaris**, **Feralas** and **Moonglade** in Kalimdor; the **Arathi Highlands**, the **Alterac Mountains**, the **Badlands**, the **Searing Gorge**, **The Hinterlands**, the **Swamp of Sorrows**, the **Blasted Lands**, the **Burning Steppes** and the **Eastern Plaguelands** in the Eastern Kingdoms, and the Alliance side of **Stranglethorn Vale**. Each has its own lines for the Alliance and for the Horde (Moonglade only for the Alliance so far; the Eastern Plaguelands got their Horde lines in W04), and towns and sites of their own (Nijel's Point, Shadowprey Village, Theramore Isle, Brackenwall Village, Gadgetzan, Feathermoon Stronghold, Camp Mojache, Refuge Pointe, Hammerfall, Kargath, Thorium Point, Aerie Peak, Revantusk Village, Stonard, Nethergarde Keep, Morgan's Vigil, Flame Crest, Light's Hope Chapel...). It also gave the nineteen five-player **dungeons** their first lines, from Ragefire Chasm and the Deadmines to Stratholme and Scholomance: they belong to their continent, since a dungeon has no area of its own.

The fourth wave (W04) reached the endgame zones: **Western Plaguelands** in the Eastern Kingdoms and **Felwood**, **Un'Goro Crater**, **Silithus** and **Winterspring** in Kalimdor, each with its own lines for the Alliance and for the Horde, and the Horde side of the **Eastern Plaguelands**, with their towns and sites (Chillwind Camp, the Bulwark, Andorhal, Caer Darrow, Talonbranch Glade, Bloodvenom Post, the Emerald Sanctuary, Marshal's Refuge, Cenarion Hold, Everlook...). It gave the **raids** (Molten Core, Blackwing Lair, Zul'Gurub, Onyxia's Lair, the Ruins of Ahn'Qiraj, Ahn'Qiraj, Naxxramas) and the **battlegrounds** (Warsong Gulch, Arathi Basin, Alterac Valley) their first lines, in the dungeon packs of their continent, more lines to **Orgrimmar** and **Stormwind City** (among them what their innkeepers, merchants, guards, trainers and flight masters say), and more of the general lines every character can hear anywhere.

The fifth wave (W05) filled four capitals: **Ironforge**, **Darnassus**, **Thunder Bluff** and **Undercity** now have lines for every moment of city life, from the districts, the inns and the flights to the weather, the hours and what their innkeepers, merchants, guards, trainers and flight masters say. It also gave the **warrior** and the **rogue** many more lines of their own, in the fight and between fights; like every class line, only the lines of your own character's class are kept in memory.

The sixth wave (W06) gave the **priest**, the **hunter**, the **warlock** and the **mage** many more lines of their own: the priest's heals, wards and resurrections, the hunter's aspects, bandages and companion, the warlock's demon, soul shards and the health it pours into its servant, the mage's study and the wit it lends to others. It also added lines for the healing of the **paladin**, the **shaman** and the **druid**, for healing in general, and for each race's way of mending. The lines fit the gesture that sets them off: a bandage line speaks on a bandage, a potion line on a potion, and a line to the one you heal only when you have a friend in your sights.

The seventh wave (W07) did the same for the **shaman**, the **paladin** and the **druid**: their thoughts on the road, their quests, their fights, and above all their heals and blessings. A healer now hears different lines when it mends itself and when it mends someone else: a line that speaks to the one you heal is said only when the spell went to them, never when you drink a potion or heal yourself with a friend in your sights. A few more lines cover anyone healing or readying themselves, and the way of each race.

Like every regional line, they are prepared by themselves when you enter their **area** (a little at a time over a few frames, so the game does not stutter) and leave memory when you leave it: Durotar and Orgrimmar are one area, Elwynn Forest and Stormwind City another, and so Dun Morogh with Ironforge, Teldrassil with Darnassus, Mulgore with Thunder Bluff and Tirisfal Glades with Undercity; each W02, W03 and W04 zone is an area of its own. The pack ids are `kalimdor_durotar`, `kalimdor_orgrimmar`, `eastern_elwynn`, `eastern_stormwind`, `eastern_dunmorogh`, `eastern_ironforge`, `eastern_tirisfal`, `eastern_undercity`, `kalimdor_mulgore`, `kalimdor_thunderbluff`, `kalimdor_teldrassil`, `kalimdor_darnassus` and, since W02, `kalimdor_barrens`, `kalimdor_darkshore`, `kalimdor_ashenvale`, `kalimdor_stonetalon`, `kalimdor_thousandneedles`, `eastern_lochmodan`, `eastern_westfall`, `eastern_redridge`, `eastern_duskwood`, `eastern_silverpine`, `eastern_wetlands`, `eastern_hillsbrad`, `eastern_stranglethorn` and, since W03, `kalimdor_desolace`, `kalimdor_dustwallowmarsh`, `kalimdor_moonglade`, `kalimdor_azshara`, `kalimdor_tanaris`, `kalimdor_feralas`, `eastern_arathi`, `eastern_alterac`, `eastern_badlands`, `eastern_searinggorge`, `eastern_hinterlands`, `eastern_swampofsorrows`, `eastern_blastedlands`, `eastern_burningsteppes` and `eastern_easternplaguelands` and, since W04, `eastern_westernplaguelands`, `kalimdor_felwood`, `kalimdor_ungorocrater`, `kalimdor_silithus` and `kalimdor_winterspring`; the dungeons, raids and battlegrounds are in `kalimdor_dungeons` and `eastern_dungeons`, with their continent (`/au packs` lists them while you are in their area). The other zones keep the general lines for now. The names of the subzones are those of the English client and are still to be confirmed in game: if a place never gets its lines, [[report it|Reporting-Bugs-and-Feedback]] with what `/au place` shows there.

## Enabling the verses pack

The verses pack is optional. To use it:

1. Copy the `AzerothUnspoken_Versi` folder into `AddOns`, as described below.
2. In game, `/au packs` should list `Verses: literary forms (prototype) (versi) - on`.
3. Type `/au event lore_test` to see one of its pieces straight away.

To stop it without uninstalling, type `/au pack versi off`.

## Installing a pack

1. Close the game.
2. Copy the pack's folder (for example `AzerothUnspoken_Example`) into `<WoW>\_forever_\Interface\AddOns`, next to the `AzerothUnspoken` folder.
3. Start the game. On the character selection screen, click **AddOns** and make sure both **Azeroth Unspoken** and the pack are ticked.
4. In game, type `/au packs` to check that the pack is listed.

Only install packs from people you trust, as with any addon.

To remove a pack, delete its folder from `AddOns`. To update it, replace the folder.

## Turning packs on and off

You do not need to uninstall a pack to stop using it.

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `/au packs` | lists every pack, with its id and whether it is on or off |
| `/au pack <id> on` | turns a pack on |
| `/au pack <id> off` | turns a pack off |
| `/au stats` | how many lines are available in your language, per event |
| `/au validate` | checks all packs for mistakes and prints a summary |
| `/au validate full` | prints every problem found |
| `/au validate <id>` | checks one pack only |

Use the **id** shown by `/au packs`, for example:

```
/au packs
/au pack example off
```

Turning a pack off is remembered for your whole account. Built-in packs can be turned off too, if you want a narrower narrator.

## What you will see

`/au packs` prints one line per pack:

```
Esempio: pesca e luoghi (example) - on
```

The pack's name is shown as its author wrote it, which may be in another language. The id in brackets is what you type.

`/au validate` prints a summary such as this one (your numbers depend on the packs you have):

> Validator: 1005 lines in 15 packs, 2109 variants: 0 errors, 3 warnings.

**Errors** usually mean a line will not work; **warnings** are smaller things, such as a line that is a bit long or an Italian line that agrees a word in the masculine without a gender placeholder. If a pack shows errors, tell its author. If a built-in pack shows errors, please [[report it|Reporting-Bugs-and-Feedback]].

## Two packs, one entry

Packs can add scripts of NPC dialogues, NPCs, places and stories, and each one has a **key** (an archetype such as `innkeeper`, an NPC number, the name of a place, the id of a story). A pack that uses a key another pack already holds **does not silently replace it**:

- by default the new entry is **refused** and the one already there stays. `/au validate` shows a `keyCollision` error that names both packs, and `/au debug` lists the same notice;
- a pack can ask to replace an entry on purpose (the author writes `override = true`). It then replaces it, and `/au validate` shows a `keyOverride` warning and `/au debug` a notice, so you can see which pack changed which entry.

If the base innkeeper suddenly behaves differently after you install a pack, look for a `keyOverride` warning. To get the base behavior back, remove or turn off that pack (`/au pack <id> off`, then `/reload`). Pack authors: see the pack guide.

## Writing your own pack

Writing packs is for people who like to tinker. The complete guide, with examples, lives in the repository: [PACK_FORMAT.md](https://github.com/Finland6738/azeroth-unspoken/blob/main/AzerothUnspoken/PACK_FORMAT.md). Good lines follow a few quality rules, described in [CONTRIBUTING.md](https://github.com/Finland6738/azeroth-unspoken/blob/main/CONTRIBUTING.md).

## Tips

- After installing a pack, use `/au validate <id>` once to make sure it loaded cleanly.
- If something strange appears after installing a pack, turn the pack off with `/au pack <id> off` and see if the problem goes away.
